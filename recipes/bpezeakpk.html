A delicious Haitian special made mainly with plantains and onions.

Bannann Peze ak Pikliz (Haitian Fried Plantains with Spicy Pickled Cabbage) is the ultimate street food staple and the most beloved side dish in Haitian cuisine. This vibrant pairing features green plantains that are sliced, twice-fried, and pressed into crispy, golden discs that are beautifully tender on the inside. To balance their savory richness, they are traditionally served with pikliz—a fiery, crunchy relish made of shredded cabbage, carrots, scotch bonnet peppers, and lime juice or vinegar. Whether enjoyed as a standalone snack or paired with fried pork (griot) or fish, this combination delivers the perfect contrast of heat, acidity, and satisfying crunch.

INGREDIENTS

  • Green Plantains: 4 large, firm green plantains (unripe).
  • Vegetable Oil: Enough for deep frying (such as canola or peanut oil).
  • Salted Water Bath: 1 cup of water mixed with 1 teaspoon of salt and 1 minced garlic clove.
  • Cabbage: 2 cups of shredded cabbage (for the pikliz).
  • Carrots: 1/2 cup of grated carrots.
  • Onion: 1/2 cup of thinly sliced onions.
  • Scotch Bonnet Peppers: 1 to 2 peppers, finely minced (adjust for heat level).
  • Vinegar and Lime Juice: 1 cup of white vinegar mixed with the juice of 1 lime.
  • Seasonings for Pikliz: 1/2 teaspoon of salt, a pinch of black pepper, and 2 whole cloves.

STEPS

  1. Make the Pikliz Base: In a large glass bowl or jar, combine the shredded cabbage, grated carrots, sliced onions, and minced scotch bonnet peppers.
  2. Pickle the Vegetables: Pour the white vinegar, lime juice, salt, black pepper, and whole cloves over the vegetable mixture, pressing down so everything is submerged, then cover and let it sit in the fridge for at least 4 hours (ideally overnight).
  3. Peel the Plantains: Cut off both ends of each green plantain, score the skin lengthwise along the ridges with a knife, and use your fingers to peel away the tough skin.
  4. Slice and First Fry: Cut the peeled plantains crosswise into thick chunks, about 1 to 1.5 inches wide, and fry them in a pot of hot vegetable oil over medium heat for about 4 to 5 minutes until they softed but do not brown.
  5. Soak and Press: Remove the softened plantains from the oil, dip them quickly into the salted garlic water bath, and press them flat using a wooden plantain press (tostonera) or the flat bottom of a heavy glass or plate.
  6. Second Fry: Return the flattened plantains into the hot oil and fry them for another 2 to 3 minutes until they turn crispy and beautifully golden brown.
  7. Drain and Season: Remove the crispy fried plantains from the oil and let them drain on paper towels, then sprinkle with a little extra salt while they are still hot.
  8. Arrange the Fried Plantains: Pile the hot, golden bannann peze onto a serving platter or individual plates while they are at their maximum crispiness.
  9. Top with Fresh Pikliz: Use a slotted spoon or fork to lift the spicy pikliz out of its jar, allowing the excess vinegar to drain slightly, and place a generous heap of it directly on top of or next to the plantains.
  10. Pair with Proteins (Optional): Serve immediately alongside classic Haitian fried meats like griot (fried pork), fried chicken, or fried fish for a complete, authentic meal.

Enjoy your dish !
