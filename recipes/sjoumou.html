SOUP JOUMOU

Image from https://www.magnific.com

A delicious Haitian soup made with vegetables and spices.

The " Soup Joumou " became a staple dish in Haiti in 1804, when the country became the first free Black republic following more than a decade of resistance and centuries of occupation by French colonizers. Long before independence dating back to the 17th century, Africans and Taínos cultivated squash for sustenance during periods of violence and domination. The word "joumou" means squash in Haitian Creole, and the vegetable forms the base of this stew, which enslaved Haitians once prepared exclusively for their French colonizers. On January 1, 1804, Marie-Claire Heureuse Félicité, wife of the revolutionary Jean-Jacques Dessalines, reclaimed joumou soup as a decolonized dish, serving it to nourish newly freed Haitians and celebrate their liberation. Today, Haitians in the diaspora uphold this tradition every New Year's Day, gathering around pots of this liberating stew. While every community, household, and matriarch prepares joumou soup in their own way, the dish's essence remains unchanged: Caribbean calabar squash, starchy vegetables, aromatic herbs, and *epis* seasoning.

INGREDIENTS

  • 900 g boneless beef stew meat, cut into 2.5 cm pieces
  • 7 tablespoons Haitian epis seasoning
  • 2 limes
  • 1 1/2 pounds calabar squash, halved and seeded
  • 1 cup leeks, diced
  • 1/2 cup celery, diced
  • 1/2 cup onion, diced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1/2 beef bouillon cube
  • 2 chicken bouillon cubes
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 6 whole cloves
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1 cup carrots, sliced into rounds
  • 1 cup Yukon Gold potatoes, diced
  • 1 1/2 cups chopped cabbage
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper, stem removed and pierced
  • 8 cups chicken broth or water
  • 1 box mezzo rigatoni pasta
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • Avocado oil
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons adobe seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground black pepper
  • 1/2 cup water

STEPS

  1. Rinse the beef in cold water, drain well, and place it in a large bowl. Rub the meat with 4 tablespoons of spices, the juice of one lime, and 2 teaspoons of salt. Cover and let marinate for at least an hour, or preferably overnight in the refrigerator.
  2. Preheat the oven to 200°C (gas mark 6-7). Arrange the squash halves, cut-side up, on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Drizzle with avocado oil, season with salt and pepper, and roast for 30 to 45 minutes, or until tender.
  3. Let the squash cool slightly, then scoop out the flesh and blend until smooth. You should have about 4 cups; set aside.
  4. Allow the marinated beef to come to room temperature and pat it dry with paper towels.
  5. Heat a large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot over high heat. Add 1 tablespoon of avocado oil. Brown the beef on all sides in batches, adding more oil if necessary. Remove the beef and set aside.
  6. Reduce the heat to medium. Add a little more avocado oil if needed, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
  7. Add the leeks, celery, and onion; sauté until tender and translucent, about 5 minutes. Stir in the tomato paste, beef and chicken bouillon cubes, cayenne pepper, cloves, ginger, allspice, 1/2 teaspoon of salt, 1/2 teaspoon of black pepper, and the remaining 3 tablespoons of spices. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until the mixture is fragrant.
  8. Stir in the tomato paste, beef and chicken bouillon cubes, cayenne pepper, cloves, ginger, allspice, 1/2 teaspoon salt, 1/2 teaspoon black pepper, and the remaining 3 tablespoons epis. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until fragrant.
  9. Return the seared beef to the pot. Add the squash puree and chicken broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat, cover, and simmer for about 1½ hours, or until the beef is tender. Check periodically to ensure the stew does not stick to the bottom of the pot.
  10. Add the Scotch bonnet pepper, carrots, potatoes, and cabbage. Simmer uncovered for 20 to 30 minutes, or until the vegetables are tender.
  11. Taste and adjust the seasoning with salt, pepper, or extra spices if needed.
  12. To prepare the dumplings, mix the flour, adobe seasoning, salt, and black pepper in a bowl. Stir in the water until a firm dough forms.
  13. Cut the dough into strips 5 cm long and 1.25 cm wide using kitchen shears or your hands. Drop the dumplings directly into the simmering broth. Cook until they rise to the surface, about 3 to 5 minutes.
  14. Meanwhile, bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta according to the package instructions.
  15. Remove the Scotch bonnet pepper from the soup. Stir in the lime juice.
  16. Serve the Soup Joumou with the cooked pasta mixed in. Enjoy with warm bread or fried plantains.

Enjoy your dish !
