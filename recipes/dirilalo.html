A delicious Haitian dish made mainly with rice, vegetables and crabs.

White rice with *lalo* and crab (*diri blan ak lalo ak sirik*) is one of the most iconic and flavorful gems of Haitian cuisine. Originating primarily from the Artibonite department, this comforting dish combines jute leaves (*lalo*)—slow-simmered with blue crab, beef, or other meats—traditionally served over a bed of fragrant white rice.

INGREDIENTS

  • Lalo (Jute Leaves): 2 lbs of fresh, frozen, or dried jute leaves (finely chopped).
  • Blue Crab (Sirik): 4 to 6 fresh crabs, cleaned, cracked, and halved.
  • Beef or Pork (Optional): 1 lb of beef chuck, oxtail, or smoked pork belly cut into bite-sized pieces.
  • Haitian Épis: 4 tablespoons of traditional green seasoning base.
  • Citrus: Juice of 2 limes or sour oranges for cleaning the meat and seafood.
  • Aromatics: 1 medium onion (chopped), 3 garlic cloves (minced), and 3 sprigs of fresh thyme.
  • Spices: 2 whole cloves, 1 teaspoon of black pepper, and salt to taste.
  • Scotch Bonnet Pepper: 1 whole pepper (kept intact for aroma).
  • Oil or Butter: 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil or butter.
  • White Rice: 3 cups of long-grain white rice.
  • Water or Broth: 6 cups of water (divided for the stew and the rice).

STEPS

  1. Clean and Marinate the Proteins: Thoroughly wash the crab pieces and meat with lime juice, rinse with hot water, and marinate with the Haitian Épis, black pepper, garlic, and thyme for at least 2 hours.
  2. Sear and Cook the Meat: Heat oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot, add the marinated meat (reserve the crab for later), and sear until browned on all sides.
  3. Simmer the Meat: Pour in a cup of water, cover the pot, and let the meat simmer on medium heat until tender, adding water as needed and reserving the cooking broth.
  4. Cook the Lalo Leaves: Add the chopped lalo leaves and chopped onions directly into the same pot with the meat, pouring in the reserved cooking broth and a bit of extra warm water.
  5. Slow Cook the Stew: Cover and simmer the lalo on low heat for 30 to 45 minutes, stirring occasionally until the leaves soften into a rich, silky texture.
  6. Add the Crab: Gently fold the marinated crab pieces and the whole Scotch Bonnet pepper into the stew, then let it simmer for an additional 15 to 20 minutes until the crab turns bright orange and cooks through.
  7. Prepare the Rice Base: In a separate pot, bring water to a boil with a tablespoon of oil, minced garlic, and salt.
  8. Steam the White Rice: Add the washed white rice to the boiling water, let the liquid evaporate uncovered on medium-high heat, then cover tightly and reduce the heat to the lowest setting to steam for 20 minutes.
  9. Plate the Base: Fluff the steamed white rice with a fork and spoon a generous bed of it onto the center of a shallow dinner plate or bowl.
  10. Layer the Lalo and Crab: Ladle a rich portion of the hot lalo stew directly over the white rice, ensuring every plate gets a fair share of the tender meat and flavorful crab pieces.
  11. Add the Traditional Sides: Garnish the side of the plate with fresh, thick slices of ripe avocado and a spoonful of spicy, pickled Haitian pikliz.
  12. Optional Bean Sauce Pairing: Drizzle a side of warm Haitian black bean sauce (sòs pwa nwa) around the rice for the ultimate authentic Artibonite culinary experience.

Enjoy your dish !
